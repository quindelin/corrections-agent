## The text

```text
Subject: Question about bulk access to plan review comments on Property Research Online

Hello,

My name is [Your full name]. [Entity name] builds software that helps architects and permit expediters respond to plan review correction comments, and intends to charge for it. The records would be used to test and improve that software on real comments.

I'm writing about public plan review comments on Property Research Online. To test the software on real comments rather than ones we wrote, we would like to use them. I'm asking you rather than assuming.

So far we have read about five review records and a few related pages, one at a time, to see what the site holds. We haven't crawled it. Your disclaimer page describes the data as for personal use and doesn't address automated access or a company's use, so I'm asking before doing anything more.

**Three questions:**

1. Is there a bulk export or data extract of review comments for commercial and residential building permits?
2. If not, would the City accept slow automated retrieval from the site, at a rate and hours you set? We would not do this without a yes.
3. What conditions apply to using the comments, such as attribution or no redistribution?

We would remove personal names and contact details before testing, and we wouldn't republish the comments without asking.

If the answer to these is no, I'd make a small public records request instead. A short answer is plenty, and I'm happy to talk by phone.

Thank you.

[Your full name]
[Entity name, if any]
[Mailing address]
[Email]
[Phone]
```

## Before you send

- Open the Clerk's Public Records page. Confirm the email and whether the City requires its form. If so, copy this text into it.
- Tick commercial on the form and complete the commercial fee form. Ask for the amount first, because Arizona allows a charge for commercial value.
- Read the PRO disclaimer yourself before sending the permission ask.
- Decide whether the records request goes to the Clerk or to TDC Online.
- The permission ask promises to remove names and not to republish without asking. Send it only if you'll keep that.

## Ask a lawyer

- Check the wording of the commercial purpose statement, and how the fee rule in A.R.S. 39-121.03 would apply.
- Does 'for personal use' on the PRO disclaimer limit a company's testing use?
- Have a lawyer check the statement's wording. A commercial requester may pay a share of the cost of obtaining the record, the labor cost of reproducing it, and its commercial value as determined by the public body (39-121.03(A)). Misusing a record or misstating the purpose can bring damages of three times the fee (39-121.03(C)). Also ask whether a City form must be used for the statement.
