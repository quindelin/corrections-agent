# Redline pricing and onboarding: what to charge, how to sell it, and how to get from pay to value

1. **Price:** open at **$59 per permit, all correction rounds included**, with a free preview, a 14-day refund and a $490 pack of 10 for expediters. No seats, subscription or success fee.
2. **Why not higher:** the estimated value is about $159 to $480 per letter, but a direct competitor already sells this job for [$19 to $69 per letter][CL1], with no signup and a free preview.
3. **Flow:** the fastest path reverses the founder's idea. Run the letter before payment, so paying only unlocks finished work. Pay-to-value should take seconds, and the letter file is the only required input.
4. **Margin:** about 94% on Haiku 4.5 (estimated). Model cost is not the constraint. Whole drawing sets sent through the agent loop and human support time are: 15 minutes of support per sale cuts margin to about 47%.
5. **Biggest caveat:** **no customer has been interviewed**, so every figure is a published anchor or an estimate, not measured willingness to pay. Redline is also unproven on real letters, and an Illinois seal question needs a lawyer.

*Research date 2026-10-31. Market research, not legal advice. Source tags: **P** primary (government, statute, or a vendor's own price page for its own price), **V** vendor or trade-group claim, **S** secondary report, **E** search excerpt only, because I could not open the page. ## Value calculation

All of this is estimated. No firm has measured its hours with Redline.

**Firm or expediter, per letter.** Hours saved means drafting and lookup time only. Redline does not revise drawings.

| Input | Low | Base | High | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Comments per letter | 8 | 12 | 25 | Est., from a vendor's [10 to 30][CL1] and Honolulu's [23.5 falling to 7.7][CO3] per permit |
| Minutes per comment by hand | 10 | 20 | 30 | Base is [vendor's 20][CL1]; others est. |
| Minutes per comment to review a draft | 5 | 5 | 7 | Est. Most uncertain input |
| **Hours saved** | **0.67** | **3.0** | **9.6** | Calculated |
| Staff cost, $39.32 × 1.35 burden (burden est.) | $53/h | $53/h | $53/h | [ST1] |
| **Saving at staff cost** | **$35** | **$159** | **$509** | Cash cost of the time |
| Billing rate | $125 | $160 | $250 | [ST4], [ST2], [ST3] |
| **Value at billing rate** | **$83** | **$480** | **$2,396** | Only if freed hours get billed |

For an expediter, 3 hours at $50 to $250 is $150 to $750 of labor. An expediter who bills hourly may gain capacity rather than savings, and licensed items still need an architect.

**Owner, per round.** A $600,000 carried balance (est.) at 8.5% costs about $140 per day, close to BIAW's $159. If Redline shortened the applicant's turn by 3 to 7 days, that would be about $420 to $1,110. **No evidence shows that it would**, because consultants and drawing revisions may be the real bottleneck. Chicago reported [50 of 71 days][OW4] with the applicant, but that does not show how much time is drafting. This value goes to the owner, not the buyer, so use it to justify a pass-through rather than to set the price.

**What it means for price.** $59 is about 37% of the base staff-cost saving and 12% of the billing value. On a small letter with a fast reviewer it exceeds the staff-cost saving ($35), so the refund matters. Value supports more than $59, but a buyer compares against the $39 Comment Letter charges for a 12-comment letter, not against their hours.

## How firms buy

**The market is many very small firms.** AIA counts [more than 19,000 US firms][AIA1], about 75% with fewer than 10 employees: 28% sole practitioners, 32% with two to four, 15% with five to nine (2024 report, 2023 data). That is roughly 14,250 firms (calculated), but small firms' share of billings fell by half from 2015 to 2023, and residential is [about 20% of design revenue][AIA1]. A 2026 edition was not found.

**Chicago alone cannot carry this price.** The [city data][LOCAL2] in this repo shows 7,265 Standard Plan Review and Direct Developer permits issued in 22 months, about 3,960 per year. Even if every one drew a letter and bought, that is about $234,000 per year at $59. At 10% capture it is about $23,000. Many of those are larger projects than the target. Chicago is where to learn, and the real market needs more cities.

| Model | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Per project** | Zermit sells per permit; GreenLite prices per project; InspectMind per check | **Use it.** It maps to a project number, and rounds are the real unit of pain |
| Per letter | Comment Letter | Workable, but it charges again each round. Competing on rounds is an opening |
| Prepaid pack | Zermit's 10-permit plan is the closest example, though it is monthly | **Use for expediters**, who have recurring volume |
| Subscription | UpCodes, Monograph, PlanChecker AI | Not yet. Letter frequency per firm is not found, and a firm with a few letters a year won't subscribe |
| Per seat | The reviewer must be able to open a link. Comment Letter shares by link; InspectMind's volume offer has no seat cost | **No.** It would tax the architect sign-off |
| Success-based | No competitor found. Chicago Standard Plan Review averages [117 days][LOCAL2] | **No.** The outcome depends on the city and architect, cash arrives months later, and it rewards pushing items through |

**Pass-through to clients is plausible but unproven.**

- Reimbursables are generally [costs recovered under the contract, on top of the fee][VG1], with a markup set by that contract (V). Approval fees appear in [published architect clauses][CT1], which do not list software (S).
- **I found no source showing that AI tools or similar software pass through as reimbursables.** Treat "the client pays" as a hypothesis (experiment 3).
- A per-permit receipt carrying the permit number makes this easier to try. Stripe's [post-payment invoice][SP1] costs 0.4%, capped at $2.
- Billing model changes the incentive. AIA's 2018 data showed [hourly as the largest share at smaller firms][AM1]. On a fixed fee, saved hours are margin. On hourly work, saved hours can reduce billings. AIA itself [urges value-based pricing][AIA3] for this reason.
- Whether correction work is billable at all depends on the contract. I found no reliable source on that.

**Who decides and what stops them.** At a firm of one to four people the owner likely decides and pays by card. That is my inference from firm size, not a sourced finding. $59 is below one Bluebeam seat. The blockers are not budget:

| Blocker | What the sources say | What Redline should do |
|---|---|---|
| Liability | AIA says architects [stay accountable][AIA3] for AI-assisted work and outputs must be validated. AIA Trust reports negligence is [judged on outcomes and responsibility][IN5] whatever the tool. Redline can't take this risk away | Sell drafts and holds, never compliance |
| Insurers | [80% of 15 insurers][IN2] see AI adoption as a possible disruption (Feb 2026). Brokers report [broad AI exclusions on architects' policies, not yet widespread][IN3] (May 2026, paywalled preview only). Berkley uses [absolute AI exclusions including E&O][IN4], per a policyholder law firm, not specific to design firms | Tell buyers to check renewal wording with their broker. Offer a one-page description of what the tool does and doesn't do |
| Seal, Illinois | See below | Keep the architect in control and export a record |
| Confidentiality | AIA says [not to upload confidential data to unvetted systems][AIA3]. AIA Trust says [a paid plan isn't automatically private][IN6]. B101-2017 has [confidentiality terms at 10.8][CT2] (S). In AIA's 2025 study, [data use and security were top concerns][AIA2] | Require only the letter, no drawings. Publish retention, subprocessors and no-training terms |
| Client disclosure | AIA [recommends disclosing AI use][AIA3] to clients | Provide a one-sentence disclosure the firm can reuse |
| Low adoption | [27% of small firms][AIA1] reported AI use (2024); [8% of firms had implemented it][AIA2] (2025, different definition) | Assume no AI policy exists and no IT buyer |

**Learning conflicts with privacy.** Redline's pitch is that it improves from each run and from architects' rejection notes. Comment Letter [promises files never train AI models][CL1]. Anthropic [does not train on commercial API data by default][AN4], but Redline's own rule rewriting is a separate question. Make learning opt-in, strip project details from learned rules, and say so plainly. Confirm TrueFoundry's and TrueForge's data terms, which I did not research.

**Illinois seal rules (flag for a lawyer).** Under [225 ILCS 305/14][IL1] (FindLaw copy, current to Jan 1, 2025; the official site failed to load):

- An architect may seal only work prepared by or under their responsible control, defined as the control and detailed knowledge a careful architect ordinarily keeps during preparation.
- The statute says "Merely reviewing or reviewing and correcting" work prepared outside the office's regular employment does not qualify.
- Where others outside regular employment prepare work under the architect's control, the architect must keep records for at least 5 years.

Whether AI drafts fall within this is **not answered by anything I found**. It matters because "the agent drafts, the architect signs off" could look like review after the fact. NCARB [says licensed practitioners must stay in responsible control and understand their tools][NC1], and will reassess responsible control with boards (updated Apr 2026). I found no Illinois board statement on AI, and could not open [68 Ill. Adm. Code 1150][IL3]. The safer design has the architect directing the work: setting instructions, editing, approving item by item, and exporting the session record.

**Free letter or guarantee?**

- **Free preview for everyone.** It costs cents and is now the category norm ([Comment Letter][CL1], [InspectMind][IM1]).
- **Refund for 14 days, no questions.** Parity, not a differentiator. Stripe [keeps its fee on refunds][SP6], about $2.
- **A fully free letter only for early design partners**, in exchange for opt-in feedback and a real letter. Those letters are worth more than the fee, since [real letters are this repo's top need][LOCAL1].
- **No guarantee of city acceptance.** It can't be controlled, and it creates liability.

### Questions for a lawyer

1. Is a response letter filed with the Department of Buildings a "technical submission" under 225 ILCS 305/14?
2. Can AI-drafted content be under "responsible control", and what must the architect do during preparation?
3. Does giving an unlicensed expediter code-based drafts risk unlicensed practice, given that [practice includes consultation][IL2]? Does Redline's own offering?
4. Would filing for customers require Chicago [expediter licensing][EX7]? This plan assumes Redline never files.
5. Terms: disclaimers, liability cap, refund wording, and whether Redline needs its own E&O cover.
6. Rights to learn from customer letters and notes when the underlying project is confidential.

## Recommended flow

**Take payment second.** The founder asked for a button that gets someone running right after paying. Better: the work is done before the button appears. A run cost [about $0.02][LOCAL3] and about 18 seconds per synthetic letter, so running first risks cents, while the buyer sees their own letter handled before deciding.

The category supports this. Value-before-pay is what [Comment Letter][CL1] and [InspectMind][IM1] do; PlanChecker AI, UpCodes and ArchiSnapper use trials; PermitFlow, GreenLite and Monograph route through sales. Agency data shows [card-required trials convert better once started but draw fewer starts][BM1] (V, agency clients, subscriptions, so only directional). Self-serve sellers in this exact category skip the trial, and Redline should too.

**Minimum information:** the letter file. Everything else is detected, optional, or collected by Stripe.

| # | What the customer sees | What happens | Target time (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One drop zone: "Drop your correction letter." No signup | Accept PDF, Word, photo | 10 s |
| 2 | Comments appear as they are read | Extract comments, city, permit number, date | 30 to 90 s |
| 3 | One question, only if needed | If the city isn't covered, stop, with nothing to pay. If the code edition is unclear, ask the application date | 10 s |
| 4 | **Free preview:** every comment in the letter's numbering, sorted by route, with a count of items held for a licensed professional, plus 3 full drafts with citations | Whole letter already run | Instant |
| 5 | **"Unlock this permit, $59"** | [Payment Link][SP3] with `client_reference_id` set to the report ID. Cards and wallets only, because [bank debits can take 2 to 14 days][SP2]. [Custom field][SP4] for project or permit number; terms checkbox. Stripe collects email | 30 s with a saved wallet |
| 6 | Back on the report, unlocked | [Redirect with the session ID][SP2], verify on the server, webhook `checkout.session.completed` as backstop | Under 5 s |
| 7 | All drafts, citations, Word export, receipt | Emailed magic link replaces an account | Instant |
| 8 | "Send held items to your architect": one email field | Reviewer link, free, no seat. The existing review queue | 15 s |
| 9 | Next round: drop the new letter | Matched on permit number, no charge | Same as step 2 |

Don't use a [card hold][SP5]. It complicates a flow that needs none, lasts about 7 days and excludes ACH. A static Payment Link is enough for one price and the pack. Move to the Checkout Sessions API only when price or checkout data must vary per report. Later, offer [Stripe's saved-payment option][SP4] for repeat buyers. Add project details and drawings only when a specific draft needs them.

### What can't be fully automated

| Situation | What the product should do |
|---|---|
| **Licensed judgment** | Never automate. Show the item as held with notes and no compliance claim. Mark the export as draft until the named professional approves. Redline should not supply its own reviewer: an outside reviewer may not satisfy the Illinois rule above, and it would [wreck the margin](#gross-margin) |
| **Buyer has no licensed person** (some expediters) | Deliver the rest, and require the architect-of-record's email to clear holds |
| **City not covered** | Say so before payment. Take no money. Offer a waitlist, and with consent keep the letter as a demand signal. Don't draft from model code, because a citation only counts if it's in that place's index. This is a real gap against Comment Letter's coverage claims |
| **Code edition uncertain** | Ask the application date. [The 2026 Chicago ordinance][LOCAL1] becomes mandatory for projects starting Dec 1, 2026 and isn't indexed: hold those letters and don't charge until it is |
| **Unreadable scan or miscount** | Show extracted comments beside the source and let the user fix them before paying |
| **Comment looks wrong** | There is [no dispute route yet][LOCAL1]. Send it to licensed review as a possible dispute |
| **Other agencies or zoning** | Route out with a named next step, as `other_agency` does now |
| **Sheet references without drawings** | Mark sheets as "from the letter" and don't imply they were checked |

## Recommended pricing

**Before charging anyone:** fix the [remaining unsafe route][LOCAL1], run at least 3 real letters past a licensed architect with zero unsafe routes, and confirm the Certified Plan Corrections rules against the city's pages. Today's 95.57 means agreement with an answer key this project wrote.

| Offer | Price | Who | What's included |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preview | Free | Everyone | Whole-letter triage plus 3 full drafts |
| **Permit** | **$59** (shown as a founding price) | Small firms | Every correction letter on that permit for 12 months, up to 40 comments per letter, unlimited reviewers, Word export, audit record |
| **Permit pack** | **$490 for 10** ($49 each), no expiry | Expediters and busier firms | Same, drawn down per permit |
| Design partner | Free for the first letter | First 5 to 10 Chicago firms or expediters | In exchange for a real letter, reviewer notes and opt-in learning |

**Why $59:**

- **Parity at two rounds.** At Comment Letter's prices, a 12-comment first letter plus a 5-comment second letter costs $58, and a third small round brings it to $77. Redline costs more on a one-letter permit and less beyond two. Rounds are common, though the only count I have is [San Francisco's median of 3][LOCAL4]; Chicago's is not found.
- **A second market reference.** Zermit's $59.99 per permit is for a different job, but it shows per-permit pricing with corrections listed.
- **Simple to buy.** One price needs no dynamic quote, works with a static Payment Link, and makes one clean project expense.
- **Revenue isn't the goal yet.** Over the first 20 sales, the gap between $59 and $99 is $800. Real letters and reviewer notes are worth more now.
- **Room to rise.** A premium has to be earned by beating Comment Letter on Chicago accuracy, which has not been shown. Calling it a founding price leaves room to raise it.

Against what firms already spend, 10 letters a year (est., not sourced) would be at most $590, about one Bluebeam Max seat.

### Gross margin

**Measured today:** TrueForge reported [$0.197 and $0.185][LOCAL3] for 8 letters: $0.023 to $0.025 per letter, about 16,000 input and 1,700 output tokens on Haiku 4.5. That confirms the $0.02 to $0.03 figure, **but those letters have only 6 comments and no file intake.** Cost scales at about $0.004 per comment.

**Assumptions (est. unless linked):** $59 price; [Stripe 2.9% + 30¢][SP1] = $2.01; 10% refunds, so net revenue is $53.10, with the fee kept; 9 free previews per sale; 2 rounds per permit (12 and 5 comments); $0.25 hosting and email per sale. PDF pages at [1,500 to 3,000 text tokens][AN2] plus [about 1,560 image tokens][AN3]; 4-page and 2-page letters. Model prices from [Anthropic][AN1]: Haiku 4.5 $1/$5, Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10, Opus 5.5 $4/$20 per million tokens. Your gateway price wasn't checked. Excludes fixed costs such as legal, insurance and your own time.

| Scenario | Variable cost per sale | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|
| Measured cost carried forward (Haiku 4.5, text only) | $2.52 | 95.2% |
| **Base: real PDFs, Haiku 4.5** | **$2.95** | **94.4%** |
| Sonnet 5.5 throughout | $3.64 | 93.1% |
| Opus 5.5 throughout | $5.02 | 90.5% |
| Sonnet 5.5, plus 40-sheet set with [caching][AN1], 10 cited sheets tiled, previews stay on Haiku | $5.60 | 89.4% |
| Opus 5.5, 80 sheets resent uncached through a 10-turn loop, 3 rounds | $95.38 | **-80%** |
| Base plus 15 min of human support at $100 per hour | $27.95 | 47.4% |
| Base plus Redline paying an architect 1 hour at $160 | $162.95 | **-207%** |

The pack at $49 comes out near 95% on the same base assumptions, with 5 free previews per permit.

**What moves it:**

- **Drawings are the only model risk.** A large sheet is [downscaled to about 1,560 tokens][AN3], which probably makes small text illegible, so reading it likely needs tiles (my estimate: 12 per sheet). Extract once, cache, send only cited sheets, cap sheets per permit, and keep drawings out of the free preview.
- **Support and refunds matter more than tokens.** Keep a hard limit on free human help.
- **Stronger models are affordable** if evals show they earn it. [Haiku 5.5][AN1] lists at a tenth of Haiku 4.5's input price for prompts under 100,000 tokens, though margin would barely change and it would need re-evaluating.
- **A lower price is viable.** At $39 the same base gives about 93%, so margin won't block a cut if experiment 1 calls for it.

## Experiments

The samples below are too small for statistical proof. Treat results as direction.

**1. Real letters at randomized prices (2 weeks; under $20 plus Stripe fees).** Run the live flow on real letters from 20 to 30 Chicago firms and expediters. After the preview, each person consistently sees $39, $59 or $99. Paying is the measure, not stated interest.
- If $99 converts at least half as well as $59: raise the price.
- If $59 converts well below $39: drop to $39.
- If almost nobody pays at any price, the problem is trust or quality, not price. Ask why before changing anything.

**2. Blind head-to-head with one architect (1 week; about $100 to $200 in reports plus 1 to 2 architect hours, about $160 to $500).** Run 3 letters through Comment Letter and Redline, then have a licensed architect score both blind: citation accuracy, unsafe routing, minutes to fix. Use letters you have permission to upload, or synthetic ones, and read Comment Letter's terms first (I did not find them). This also tests whether it really covers Chicago.
- Clearly better on Chicago: test $99.
- Tied: stay at $59.
- Behind: fix the product before selling harder.

**3. Who pays, and do expediters want packs (runs alongside; about $0).** Add a required [checkout dropdown][SP4]: firm overhead, billed to client, not sure. Separately offer the pack by link to 10 licensed Chicago expediters, using the city's public [license lookup][EX7].
- If 30% or more say billed to client: lead with the receipt and project line.
- If 2 or more of 10 buy a pack: keep it. If none do, remove it and offer per-permit only.

Also, ahead of any launch, have one design partner ask their broker whether the tool raises any issue at renewal. That answer could matter more than any price test.
